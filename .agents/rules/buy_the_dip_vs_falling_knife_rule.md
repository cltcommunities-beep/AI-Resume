# 🗡️ MANDATORY RULE: "BUY THE DIP, BUT DON'T BUY A FALLING KNIFE"

## 🚨 Executive Directive
Whenever the assistant evaluates any stock entry, dip-buying consideration, or cost-averaging proposal, the assistant **MUST STRICTLY ENFORCE** the following 5 Golden Rules. No exceptions permitted.

---

## 🏛️ The 5 Golden Rules of Dip-Buying vs. Falling Knives

### 1. 🔍 Find Out WHY the Stock Dropped (Macro vs. Micro Invalidation)
* **Market-Wide Drop:** If the entire broad market is falling due to macro catalysts (e.g. CPI/PPI inflation shocks, bond yield spikes, geopolitical headlines), a fundamentally strong company with fortress cash flows is simply **"on sale."**
* **Company-Specific Deterioration:** If the drop is caused by business deterioration (e.g. loss of anchor customers, product delays like RKLB Neutron, accounting scandals, negative gross margin decay, or share dilution), **DO NOT TOUCH IT.** A deteriorating business is an active falling knife.

### 2. 📊 Check Company Fundamentals (The 4-Point Fortress Standard)
Before buying any dip, verify the 4 fundamental pillars:
* **Revenue Growth:** Real, growing top-line customer revenue (+15% to +100% YoY).
* **Healthy Cash Flow:** Positive Operating Cash Flow and Free Cash Flow (+FCF).
* **Manageable Debt:** Cash > Debt or 100% cleared by Formula 1 (Prepaids) or Formula 2 (3-Year Cash Flow).
* **Competitive Moat:** Clear structural advantage (e.g. Nvidia CUDA monopoly, Amkor TSMC CoWoS packaging, Nebius $9B pre-paid GPU clusters).

### 3. 🎯 Invest Gradually (The 3-Tranche Staged Accumulation Rule)
* **Never Buy Everything at Once:** Lump-sum entries into a falling stock are strictly prohibited.
* **Keep Cash Available:** Always divide entries into gradual, staged tranches (Tranche 1 @ S1/S2, Tranche 2 @ S3, Tranche 3 in liquid reserve).
* **Defend the Cash Buffer:** Always maintain the mandatory **$1,500 liquid dry powder buffer** in reserve so you have firepower if the market overshoots lower.

### 4. 🧮 Don't Assume a 50% Drop Means It's "Cheap" (The Downside Asymmetry Law)
* A stock that drops from $100 to $50 can still drop to $25 (another -50% loss!).
* Valuation is determined by **earnings and cash flow (P/E, FCF yield)**, NOT how far a stock has fallen from its peak.
* Never buy a stock solely because it looks "down a lot" without confirming that valuation multiples have reached historical support floors.

### 5. ⏳ Think Long-Term (Multi-Year Capital Compounding Horizon)
* **Only invest money you can leave invested for several years.**
* Never deploy emergency funds, short-term bill money, or naked margin that can trigger forced liquidation.
* Let corporate earnings, contract execution, and compound interest do the heavy lifting over months and years.
